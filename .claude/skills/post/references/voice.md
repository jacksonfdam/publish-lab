# Voice and format

Author: Jackson F. de A. Mafra (@jacksonfdam) — Senior Android / Kotlin Multiplatform engineer, Stockholm. Writes hands-on labs for other developers: a working recipe plus the explanation of *why* it works.

## Tone
- Practitioner to practitioner. Assume the reader can run a terminal and read code; do not explain what Docker or Gradle is.
- Concrete over abstract. Prefer "the build went from 14 min to 6" to "significantly faster".
- Honest about what didn't work. A gotcha section is more valuable than a victory lap.
- Plain English, short sentences, active voice. Light humour is fine; no hype, no "game-changer", "unlock", "supercharge", "in today's fast-paced world".
- Portuguese-speaking readers are welcome but the article is in English. A closing line in Portuguese inviting people to share their experiments is on-brand.

## Recurring structure
1. Hook — the result or the itch that started it.
2. Context — what exists, what's missing, constraints.
3. The recipe — numbered steps, each independently runnable.
4. What happened — measurements, screenshots, surprises.
5. Try this next — 2–4 concrete follow-ups the reader can do.
6. Credits — people, repos, articles that made it possible (link them).
7. Footer (verbatim, adjust repo URL):

> This project is open source under a permissive license. If you use it, I only ask for credit, a shout-out, and that you share your own experiments — that's how the lab grows. Repo: <URL>

## Things to avoid
- Bullet-point walls with no prose between them.
- Restating the title in the first sentence.
- Emojis in headings. One in the teaser is the maximum.
- Claiming performance numbers the source material does not contain — leave `TODO: measure` instead.
